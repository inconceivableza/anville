How it works

Four steps, a few hours, and someone walking it with you

The steps below describe the Whatever You Do course. As an organisation we're about vocational discipleship more broadly — but this is the thing you can start today.

The calendar time is longer than the working time, deliberately. You're waiting on people — for your contacts to answer, for your coach to find an hour — and the thinking needs a few nights to settle. Nothing expires, and you can stop wherever you like and pick it up later.

STEP ONE

Find out what other people see

Sort 36 cards to map your gifts, then answer six questions in your own words. Five people who know you well answer the same questions about you, anonymously.

Most people find the gap between the two lists is the most useful thing they take away.

THROUGHOUT

Someone walking it with you

Not a step so much as the thread running through all four. You choose a coach at the start — someone who'll ask good questions rather than tell you what to do — and we send them what they need before each conversation.

You can start without one, but almost nobody finishes well alone.

Notes for implementation

The mockups are placeholders

Each is built from real design tokens and mirrors an actual screen, so the section works as-is — but they should be replaced with real screenshots once there's a build worth photographing. Keep them cropped tight and legible at half-width; full screenshots shrink to illegibility at this size, which is why these show a fragment of each screen rather than the whole thing.

Verify these against real testers before publishing. Underestimating is worse than saying nothing — someone who budgets 30 minutes and hits a 40-minute timeline exercise abandons it. If anything, round up.

Why two time figures, not one

Working time and calendar time are different questions, and conflating them is what makes people distrust the number. "6–8 weeks" alone sounds enormous; "3–4 hours" alone sounds like a lie once they're three weeks in waiting on a contact. Showing both, and explaining that the gap is deliberate, answers the real question — what are you asking of me? — honestly.

The steps changed

The previous three (noticing gifts / guided reflection / walking with others) predate the four-section structure and don't map to what someone now does. These four follow the actual journey. The coach has been pulled out of the sequence entirely and shown as a continuous thread, because that's what they are — a step implies they happen once.

Still to decide

  • Whether the three "conversations" figure should stay vague, as it was made elsewhere in the coach flow. Currently this page says three and the coach contracting says "a few" — pick one.
  • Whether the closing line ("your first sitting is twenty minutes") should link to the specific first step rather than the generic Begin.
