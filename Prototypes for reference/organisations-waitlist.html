Notes for implementation

This is demand validation, not a waitlist

The page is honest about that, and it works better for it. "We're working out whether to build this, and what it would take" makes the funding question a legitimate thing to ask rather than a sales manoeuvre — and it gives you permission to come back later and say no.

How the qualifying works

Nobody is asked how much money they have. Four softer signals do the same job:

  • Cohort size — the cleanest proxy for value. 200+ people is a different conversation from 8.
  • Timing — "within 3 months" is a real need; "just exploring" is a mailing list entry.
  • How it would be paid for — the direct question, framed so every answer is acceptable. "It would need to be free" is explicitly welcomed, which is what makes the other answers trustworthy.
  • Open to a conversation — the strongest signal on the page. Anyone selecting "we'd consider piloting it" goes to the top of the list.

Score roughly as: size × timing × funding, with the pilot option overriding everything.

The feature question earns its place twice

It qualifies, and it's genuine product research. If three quarters of respondents pick "gifts across the organisation" and almost nobody picks branding, that's your build order decided by the people who'd pay for it. Capped at three so it forces a real choice.

Honesty about what exists

The two-column panel near the top separates what's available now from what's proposed. A page that implies the admin view exists and then asks you to join a waitlist burns the exact trust you need from this audience — these are people who'd be staking their own credibility internally on recommending it.

Still to decide

  • Whether to state a price range. Naming a number filters harder and wastes less of everyone's time, but it's hard to do before knowing what you're building.
  • Whether the 86/71/100 figures can be shown as cohort-level results before any cohort has produced them. They're currently your aggregate individual numbers — the mockup implies something not yet measured at that level.
  • Whether "if enough organisations want it, it gets built" is a promise you want in writing. It's the most trust-building line on the page and also the most binding.
  • Whether individuals who've finished the course should see this at all, or only people who acted as coaches and contacts — they're the warmest possible audience for it.
