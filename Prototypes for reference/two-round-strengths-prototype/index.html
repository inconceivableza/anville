How the two-round sort works

Each of the 36 strengths is now asked twice, in two rounds, with a different angle each time. The sliders are gone. If someone's two answers clearly disagree, we ask about just those pairs. The scoring is the same as before: add up each category and show it as a share of 100.

Names

The gifts have new names in everyday words. They make sense to contacts, workplace teams and people outside church, and they don’t read like job titles. Telling someone their top gift is “Teacher” can sound like careers advice. Participants never see the original names, partly because “Apostle” is contested in some churches. The results screen just cites Ephesians 4:11–13. The originals are kept here so the build team can see the mapping.

The two profiles are the Contribution Profile (previously the APEST(d) profile), which shows the kind of difference someone tends to make, and the Professional Energy Profile , which shows which of the six stages of work give them energy. “Seer” is the fallback name for Awakener if it doesn’t land.

Each gift has two sides, and its six cards are split three and three between them: Pioneer (starting / building to last), Awakener (sensing / calling out), Persuader (reaching people unlike you / persuading), Nurturer (individuals / community), Explainer (understanding / helping others learn) and Helper (serving / organising).

What we tested

We simulated 20,000 people taking each version twice and counted how often their top gift came out the same both times. Each simulated person had a true profile across the six gifts, a personal tilt on individual cards, everyday noise, and their own habits with the scale (some sort everything high, some stay in the middle). Sliders were modelled generously, as if people could place every card exactly where they meant to.

Design Same top gift on a retake
36 cards, 5 buckets 67%
36 cards, 5 buckets, plus sliders (today) 68%
36 cards, 7 buckets 69%
72 cards, 5 buckets 75%
72 cards, 7 buckets, second wording a close paraphrase 72%
72 cards, 7 buckets, second wording from a different angle 77%
The same, with close calls (within 1 point) shown as joint top 90%

Three things stand out. Asking twice does most of the work. Seven buckets add a little. Sliders add almost nothing, because they mostly re-express the bucket choice. If the second wording is too close to the first, people answer from memory and about half the gain disappears.

Most of the wobble that's left comes from people whose top two gifts are genuinely close. Showing those as joint top, rather than picking one, gets the retake match to about 9 in 10. Around 1 person in 5 would see a joint top at the 1-point setting.

These figures compare the options with each other. They don't predict real-world numbers. Retake data from the soft launch will give the real figure, so it's worth storing every answer (see rule 8).

Writing the second round

Round 1 keeps 31 of today's wordings plus the 5 new cards described below, all describing an ability. Round 2 describes the same strength as something you'd see the person doing. Each twin follows four rules:

  • It measures the same strength, so it keeps the same gift and energy tags as its twin.
  • It avoids the key words of its twin, so people answer the words in front of them rather than remembering an earlier answer.
  • It is concrete enough to picture.
  • It contains no church language, so trusted contacts can use it as it stands.

All 36 pairs are listed at the bottom of this page.

Balancing gifts and energy types

Every card counts towards one gift and one energy type, so both profiles come from the same answers. In the current set, five gifts each had two cards of one energy type and none of another. That built links into the results that nobody had told us about. A strong Nurturer (Shepherd) came out about 5 points higher on Deliver than on Ponder from the tagging alone, and a strong Explainer (Teacher) was pushed towards Ponder and away from Rally.

Five cards have been replaced, so every gift now has exactly one card of each energy type. Each new card was chosen to replace the card whose ground is best covered by its neighbours.

Rules for the build

  1. Deal. Round 1 shows the 36 round-1 wordings in a random order. Round 2 shows the 36 round-2 wordings in a new random order. The first 8 cards of round 2 never include a twin of the last 8 cards of round 1.
  2. Buckets and points. Definitely not me 0, Not me 1, Not really me 2, In between 3, Somewhat me 4, Good at this 5, Real strength 6. Number keys 1 to 7 work on desktop. Contacts see the same buckets with “them” in place of “me”, plus “Haven’t seen this” (key 0), which isn’t scored.
  3. Score. Each gift has 12 cards (6 per round). Its score is the average points of the cards answered for it, and its share is that average divided by the sum of the six averages. For someone who answers every card, as participants do, this is the same as the gift’s points divided by their total points. The six energy types are scored the same way from the same 72 answers. Because every gift has exactly one card of each energy type, neither profile leans on the other.
  4. Round the shares to whole numbers so the six always add up to 100. Give the leftover points to the categories with the largest remainders.
  5. Joint top. Any category within 1 percentage point of the top (before rounding) is shown as joint top.
  6. Quick check. After round 2, find pairs whose two answers are 4 or more buckets apart. That always means one is on the "me" side and the other on the "not me" side. Show the biggest gaps first, at most 5. Skip the screen if there are none. People can change either answer or leave both.
  7. Round agreement. Score each round on its own as well. If both rounds put the same gift on top, say so on the results. If they disagree, say which way each leaned.
  8. Store every answer separately: pairId , version (a or b), bucket , round , answeredAt , durationMs and changedInCheck . Store “Haven’t seen this” as a bucket of null . This lets us measure real retake reliability later and spot any twin that doesn't behave like its partner. Give the card set a version number, and treat any reworded card as a new card in the data, so answers to old and new wordings are never mixed.
  9. Anchor the question. Before round 1 and under every card, ask people to think about the last 12 months. Participants are also asked to answer for what’s true now, not what they’d like to be true. Contacts are asked to think about what they’ve actually seen.
  10. Nudge rushed answers. If 5 of the last 6 cards each took under 1.2 seconds, or the last 8 cards went in the same bucket, show a one-line nudge above the next card. Show each nudge at most once per round. Never block anyone from carrying on.
  11. Talk about time, not cards. Introduce it as about 7 minutes (6 for contacts) in two quick rounds of 36. Never say “72 cards” to participants or contacts, because it sounds like far more work than it is. 
                            // answers: { "a1:a": 5, "a1:b": null, ... }  null = haven't seen
function shares(answers, items, tagOf, minSeen = 4) {
  const acc = {}; let all = 0, n = 0;
  for (const it of items) for (const v of ["a", "b"]) {
    const x = answers[`${it.id}:${v}`];
    if (x == null) continue;
    const a = (acc[tagOf(it)] ??= { sum: 0, n: 0 });
    a.sum += x; a.n++; all += x; n++;
  }
  const overall = n ? all / n : 3;
  const avg = Object.fromEntries(Object.entries(acc).map(([id, a]) =>
    [id, a.n >= minSeen ? a.sum / a.n : overall]));
  const total = Object.values(avg).reduce((s, x) => s + x, 0);
  return Object.entries(avg).map(([id, x]) => ({ id, share: x / total * 100 }));
}

Trusted contacts

Contacts do both rounds too: the same 72 cards and 7 buckets, with the question "How much is this [name]?". That keeps the comparison like for like across the whole profile.

In the simulation, three contacts doing both rounds named the right top gift 79% of the time, against 74% for three contacts doing round 2 only. Three contacts doing both rounds did as well as five doing one. The gain is lost if asking for 72 cards stops people finishing, so keep it easy to finish:

  • No quick check for contacts. Their contradictions average out across several people.
  • Tell them up front that it takes about 6 minutes.
  • Let them pause at the halfway screen and come back later.
  • The comparison unlocks once three contacts have finished. A contact only counts once they've finished both rounds. Half-finished answers aren't used.
  • With only three needed, it's easier for the participant to guess who said what. Don't show which contacts have finished. Update the combined view in batches, for example once a day, rather than the moment each contact finishes, so one person's answers never show up as a change on their own.
  • Contacts get an eighth option, “Haven’t seen this”. It isn’t scored. Each gift’s score is the average of the cards they did answer, so a work colleague who never sees someone at church doesn’t drag the Nurturer score towards the middle. If a contact has seen fewer than 4 of a gift’s 12 cards, that gift is set to their own overall average, so it neither helps nor hurts.
  • A finished response still isn’t counted if a typical card took under 1.5 seconds, if 12 or more cards in a row went in the same bucket, or if more than half the cards are “Haven’t seen this”. The participant is never told which contact was left out.

To combine contacts, take a plain average of their shares, gift by gift. Every finished response counts equally.

What this replaces

  • The fine-tune sliders. The quick check does that job for the few answers that need it.
  • The adaptive tie-breaker. Joint top handles close calls without extra questions. It can come back later if people say they want a single answer.

Still to decide

  • Whether 72 cards is too long on a phone. Track drop-off at the halfway screen during the soft launch.
  • The bucket wording, especially “Somewhat me” and “In between”.
  • The effort thresholds: 1.2 and 1.5 seconds a card, runs of 8 and 12, half the cards unseen, and 4 of 12 cards seen. They’re starting guesses. Check them against soft-launch data.
  • A few cards say “you” (“Building something that will outlast you”). Contacts read these under “How much is this Sam?”, which works, but it’s worth testing with real contacts.
  • Whether to show round agreement and the quick-check count to the participant, or only to their coach.
  • Whether the joint-top margin should be 1 point or 2.
