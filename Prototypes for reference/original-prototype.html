About
We believe everyone is designed for a purpose – and that discovering it shouldn't be left to chance.

Whatever You Do is a pathway for discerning your calling – drawing on scripture, a strengths assessment, feedback from people who know you, and the wisdom of a mentor.

We help release individuals and churches to see everyday work as ministry, and to make a kingdom impact wherever God has placed them.

How it works
01

How God has designed you

Sort through the gifts you've been given, then hold them up against what people who know you well have said. The gap between the two is usually where the surprises are.

02

The shape of your life so far

Lay your story out as a timeline and look for God's hand in it – the doors He opened or closed, the people who saw something in you, the hard seasons, the fruit that lasted.

03

Putting your calling into words

Gather it into a single sentence you could say out loud – then hold it loosely before God. There are more ways He could use this in a life than you'd think.

04

Walking it out in obedience

Steps of faith to take, skills worth growing, character worth tending, people worth learning from. Something to begin on Monday, offered as worship rather than ambition.

05

A letter to your future self

Write down what God has shown you while it is still fresh, and what you are trusting Him for. We send it back to you in a year, when you will need reminding.

Three things you'll have with you the whole way

A mentor alongside you

Two or three conversations with someone you trust. Not to tell you your calling – to help you listen for it.

People who know you

Five or more friends, family and colleagues weigh in anonymously, so you're not working this out alone.

Scripture to sit with

Honest questions rooted in scripture at every stage. No rushing, no pressure – just space to listen.

For church leaders
Help your people discover what they were made for

Whatever You Do gives church leaders a practical framework for equipping members to discern their vocational calling – in the church and in the workplace.

The problem

Most church members spend 95% of their week outside the building. They want their work to matter – to feel like it's part of God's purpose for their life. But most churches aren't equipped to help them think that through.

The result? People pursue calling alone, or don't pursue it at all. The gifts in your congregation go unidentified, undeveloped, and undeployed.

How it works for churches
01

Run a cohort

Put a group of 6–12 people through the course together over 6–8 weeks. They work through the online material individually, then gather weekly to discuss what they're discovering.

02

Pair with mentors

Each participant is paired with a mentor from the congregation – someone who commits to 2–3 conversations during the course. We provide a mentor guide to help them know what to do.

03

See the bigger picture

As a church leader, you get an aggregate view of the gifts, strengths and callings across your cohort. See where the body has capacity, where there are gaps, and where people are ready to step into new roles.

04

Deploy with confidence

Move from guesswork to evidence. Place people in roles and teams that match their actual gifts. Commission members for workplace ministry with a clear sense of where God is leading them.

Walking with a mentor

The most transformative part of this journey is having someone to walk it with; a trusted guide who can help you make sense of what you're discovering.

What does a mentor do?

They don't tell you what your calling is – they help you listen for it. A good mentor will ask the questions you haven't thought to ask, reflect back what they see in you, and hold you accountable to acting on what you discover.

This could be a pastor, a colleague you respect, a spiritual director, or simply a wise friend. Choose someone who knows you well, who you trust to be honest, and who will commit to 2–3 conversations over the coming weeks.

A letter to your future self

The task Write to yourself as you will be in twelve months' time. You have just done a lot of thinking, and most of it fades faster than you would expect. Put it down now, while it is still warm, and let it find you again when you need it.
When Israel crossed the Jordan, God told them to carry twelve stones out of the riverbed and pile them on the bank – so that when their children asked "what do these stones mean?", they would have something solid to point at. Memory needs help. This letter is your pile of stones.
Joshua 4:1–7

Things you might want to include

  • What has shifted in you – what you understand now that you did not when you started, about God, about work, about yourself.
  • Your calling, in your own words – not the polished sentence, the version you would say to a friend across a table.
  • What you are believing God for this year – name it plainly, specifically enough to actually be tested.
  • The steps you are committing to – the few that actually matter out of your growth plan, written as promises rather than intentions.
  • What might knock you off course – busyness, discouragement, someone else's opinion, an easier offer. Name it now.
  • What you want to say to yourself in a year – encouragement, a warning, a reminder of who you are.
  • A verse or prayer to carry – something to hold onto when the year gets loud.
Write it as one letter to yourself, in whatever order feels natural. Use as many of the prompts above as you like.

It’s a prototype, not a product

Almost everything cosmetic is a placeholder standing in for the real thing:

  • Colours, fonts and imagery are directional only
  • The videos are empty boxes with titles
  • The observer/feedback reports use invented data – nobody has actually assessed you
  • The impact stats on the homepage are real, but the team shown is not
  • Chunks of wording are first-draft
  • Emails aren’t sent, and the “letter to your future self” isn’t really posted anywhere yet
  • Things will break. Please tell me when they do.

Getting around

Use a desktop or laptop. It works on a phone, but – for now – several parts are much much easier to see on a bigger screen.

Getting around: use Demo mode. You do not have to work through it in order. Click the faint 🔧 Demo button in the bottom-right corner and you’ll get a panel listing every screen, grouped into Onboarding, Workbook, Assessment, Observer and End. Click any one to jump straight there. Leave “Auto-fill dummy data on jump” ticked and it will populate realistic answers so you can see what a screen looks like in use rather than empty. Untick it to see the blank version.

Saving your progress. Your work saves automatically as you go, and a small bar appears bottom-left showing when it last saved. Two caveats:

  • If you’ve opened the file directly from your computer in Safari, automatic saving is blocked by the browser. The bar will say “Auto-save off”. Use Save file to download your progress and Restore to load it back.
  • In Chrome it saves automatically, but it’s still per-browser and per-device.

Where this could go

If development partners and funding come together, here’s the roadmap beyond this prototype:

  • Shooting the videos. Every empty video box you’ve clicked on is a placeholder for real teaching content, filmed properly.
  • A path for people who aren’t Christians. Right now the whole course assumes Christian faith. Finding purpose isn’t only a Christian longing, and a version built for people exploring faith – or with none at all – could make this a genuine tool for evangelism, not just discipleship.
  • Proper theological review. This prototype has been built quickly, and the content needs testing against scripture and scrutiny from people better qualified than me before it goes anywhere near a real user.
  • Building out the mentor and church partnership model, and testing it at real scale.

This is very much a “if” rather than a “when” – which is exactly why your feedback in the next step matters so much.
