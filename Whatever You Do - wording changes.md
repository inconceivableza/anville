## Part 2: Consent, privacy and the newsletter

*This follows the UK regulator's (the ICO's) published guidance. It isn't legal advice.*

### Consent in onboarding

The long consent page becomes one step in the onboarding wizard: a single tick box, with the details in a box you can open.

> ☐ I agree to Whatever You Do storing my answers, including personal things I choose to share, such as my faith or my health, as set out in the privacy notice.

28 October: this replaces "…including anything about my faith, work or health…", so that sharing faith or health reads as your choice. The meaning is unchanged, so nobody who has already agreed is asked again.

**Why this wording, rather than "I accept the terms and conditions":** participants' answers can reveal their faith. The law treats religious belief as a special category of data, and here that means consent has to be explicit and separate. It can't sit inside general terms and conditions. The sentence above is as light as that allows.

**We don't need terms and conditions.** Data protection law doesn't require them. The consent tick plus the privacy notice covers what's needed for testing.

**The details box ("What this means")** keeps today's plain explanation of what is stored, who sees what, and how to withdraw. The parts about enrolment codes and groups are removed, since those aren't live yet. Because the same text now shows in onboarding and on the "Your consent" page, its withdrawal sentence reads: **"You can withdraw at any time from "Your consent", linked at the foot of your hub."**

**Age and name:** the "I am 18 or over" tick moves out of sign-up and into this same first step, headed **"About you"**, with the person's first name (filled in, and changeable) and the consent tick.
