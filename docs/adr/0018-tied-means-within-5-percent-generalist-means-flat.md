# Tied means within 5%; generalist means flat

After the card sort, a framework is too close to call when its leader's share is at most 5% more than another construct's share, judged on unrounded shares. Each such framework gets the tie-break's "Which is more true of you?" pairs before the results, whether one framework is tied or both. A participant is told they are a generalist only when every construct on both frameworks reads "Present" or "Strong", and then gets no pairs. Standing words, ties, flatness and the drawings all use unrounded shares.

This replaces what tickets 30a–30c built: a 4-point threshold on rounded percents, with anyone tied on both frameworks told they were a generalist and asked nothing. Under that rule almost everyone met a tie-break, and most were called generalists beside results showing a clear leader. The content owner's tie-break prototype (v2) never did that: it runs the tie-break on both frameworks, and leaves "generalist" as an open question about a person "flat on both scales".

## How the tie-break works

- **Shares.** Each of the 36 cards counts towards one role and one style, six cards per construct. A card is worth its bucket's points (10, 25, 45, 65, 85); a construct's share is its cards' points as a percent of its framework's total. An even share is 16.7%. Moving one card up one bucket moves a share by about one point.
- **Tied.** The leader and every construct its share is at most 5% more than, up to four, leader first, form the cluster. Fewer than two, and the framework is not tied. A share of 0 is never tied.
- **Pairs.** Every two constructs in a cluster make a pair, shown as one statement for each; the participant picks one or skips. Pairs never cross frameworks.
- **Rounds and budget.** Each pair is asked twice, the second time with different statements. At most 10 questions are asked in all. When that is too many, second rounds go first, then pairs, the least important first: importance is the pair's places in the sort, so 3rd against 4th goes before 1st against 2nd. When both frameworks are tied the questions alternate between them.
- **Re-weighting.** Each construct's weight is 1 + wins ÷ times asked. The cluster's combined share is shared out again by weight; constructs outside it keep their sort share.
- **Outcomes, per framework.** "Separated" when one construct comes out ahead. "Genuinely balanced" when the answers go in a circle (A beat B, B beat C, C beat A, across both rounds) or leave the best weight shared. Every pair skipped has its own wording and says nothing about the person.

**Example.** A participant's APEST(d) shares are Deacon 20.17, Evangelist 19.60, Prophet 18.16, Shepherd 17.87, Apostle 15.85 and Teacher 8.36. Deacon's share is 2.9% more than Evangelist's, so they are tied; it is 11% more than Prophet's, so Prophet is not. The participant is asked one pair twice. Teacher reads "Less used", so they are not flat, and not a generalist.

## Considered options

**4 points, the prototype's default.** Rejected: the typical gap between first and second is about two points, so in simulated sorts 96–99% of participants met a tie-break. At 5%, about 35–45% do, mostly answering two questions.

**A threshold in points rather than percent.** Equivalent in practice, since leaders sit around 18–25%: 5% is about one point. Percent was chosen because it matches how the gap is described ("11% more than").

**Generalist when tied on both frameworks.** Rejected: being tied at the top says nothing about the rest of the profile.

**Generalist when every construct is within the threshold of its leader.** Rejected: at 5% it never happens.

**Generalist as an outcome, when the tie-break comes out balanced on both.** Rejected: the cluster holds at most four constructs, so it speaks for the top of a profile, not all of it.

**One round each when both frameworks are tied**, as the prototype's code does. Rejected in favour of its notes ("second rounds are dropped first… until the total fits"): one answer would otherwise decide a framework, and under 5% the budget is rarely reached.

## Consequences

- About 0.5–2.5% of participants are told they are a generalist in simulated sorts. These are made-up sorts, so the threshold is a pathway document setting, to be tuned once real sorts are in.
- Re-weighted results keep their unrounded shares, so a re-weighted profile is drawn as precisely as a sort's. Each re-weighted share is still rounded on its own where a whole number is stored, never by giving the leader what rounding lost.
- The pair plan is worked out afresh from the stored result, so a participant told "generalist" under the old rule meets the pairs the next time they open their results.
- Observers never get the tie-break; the comparison sets the participant's re-weighted result beside the observers' sorts as sent.
